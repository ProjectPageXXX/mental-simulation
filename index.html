Mental Simulation for Vision-Language Models on Intuitive Physics

Anonymous Authors

MethodQuantitative resultsVideo results

Abstract

Vision-language models (VLMs) excel at interpreting what is visible, but can still struggle when questions depend on events that have not yet occurred. Such future-dependent questions are central to understanding the physical world, yet they require reasoning about plausible dynamics that are often absent from static or partially observed inputs. We propose a test-time approach that improves VLMs’ ability to reason about the future. Rather than relying only on a VLM’s internal text-based reasoning to extrapolate future dynamics, we augment it using a generative video model to explicitly generate multiple plausible future rollouts conditioned on the observed video. These “mental simulations” are then fed back to the VLM as additional visual context before it produces a final answer. We evaluate this simulate-then-reason framework on three intuitive physics benchmarks: Physion, CLEVRER, and real-world Physics-IQ videos. Across a diverse set of open-source and proprietary VLMs, augmenting models with imagined futures can improve prediction accuracy over both observed-only baselines and strong test-time language reasoning baselines, suggesting that allocating test-time computation to explicit video-based mental simulation can enhance multimodal reasoning about the physical world.

Method Overview

Questions about future physical events require reasoning beyond the observed frames. We use a generative video model to sample three plausible continuations conditioned on the observed video, then provide them to a VLM together with the question.

Quantitative Results

We compare observed-only inference, language-space reasoning, and visual mental simulation across eleven VLM configurations. The main evaluation contains 1,035 Physion, 1,000 CLEVRER, and 66 Physics-IQ questions.

Inference settings and evaluation details
Observed / Observed vote@3
Observed predicts the answer directly from the observed segment V0:T. As a repeated-inference control, Observed vote@3 runs the same inference three times and aggregates the resulting predictions by majority vote.
Language–Structured / Language–Future
The VLM still receives only the observed video and is prompted to reason about the scene before answering; the standard prompts request only the final answer. Language–Structured organizes the observed scene in terms of relevant objects, motions, and interactions. Language–Future asks the model to simulate how these dynamics may evolve. We evaluate both settings with a single inference and with vote@3.
Visual–Independent / Visual–Joint
We generate K = 3 imagined future rollouts and provide them as additional visual evidence. In Visual–Independent, the VLM reasons over each rollout separately and aggregates the three predictions by majority vote. In Visual–Joint, the VLM reasons jointly over all three imagined futures and produces a single answer.
